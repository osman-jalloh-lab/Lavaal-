Turn a real website or product into a pack of 10–15 short screen-recorded social clips using a local screen recorder such as Recordly. Use it for honest product demos, not mood films or fake walkthroughs.

## Rules
- Inventory first. Only demo features that exist in the current product.
- Mark anything planned, stubbed, or promised in marketing copy but not built as `NOT YET LIVE — don't record`.
- Do not invent stats, prices, testimonials, customer names, or capabilities.
- Use clearly labelled DEMO / sample data. Never show real personal data, documents, or customer records.
- Keep clips draft or preview until the owner approves each one, in their own words, before posting.
- Prefer the owner's real voice. An AI voice needs approval per asset and again before public use.
- Spend nothing, publish nothing, and install nothing on a personal machine unless the owner names that machine.
- Export recordings locally. Do not upload footage to a recorder's cloud unless the owner explicitly accepts that.

## Steps

### 1. Inventory real features
Walk the live product or current codebase (routes, templates, navigation, primary click paths). Write two lists:
- **Live and recordable:** page or screen, what the user can actually do, any labels or demo banners in the UI.
- **Not yet live:** stubs, roadmap items, marketing claims without a working screen, internal-only tools.
Note the audiences the product already serves. Do not invent a segment the product cannot show.

### 2. Pick audiences and a clip mix
Aim for 10–15 clips, 15–30 seconds each, 9:16 first. Split across the real audiences and mix formats:
- Feature demo (what it is, in one pass)
- How-to in 3 steps
- Before / after or myth vs reality, only if both sides are honest
- Day-in-the-life POV on a real flow
- Trust or "how your data is used", only if the product already says it
- Language or hook variants of a live flow, flagged if they need a reviewer
Drop a format rather than fake a screen for it.

### 3. Build a hook bank
A two-second hook per clip that works with the sound off. The first frame names the job to be done, not just the brand. Never hook on a feature that is not live.

### 4. Fill the per-clip template
For every clip:
- Number and title; audience
- Hook (first 2 seconds)
- Recorder shot list: exact page or screen, click path, zoom points
- On-screen text beats
- Optional voice-over line; optional second-language line, flagged if it needs a checker
- CTA / end card (placeholder URL until the real one is live)
- Caption and 3–5 hashtags (no banned or unproven claims)
- Which real feature it shows
- `NOT YET LIVE — don't record` if any shot depends on an unbuilt screen

### 5. Shot-list conventions
- One job per clip. Cut rather than add copy.
- Name routes or screens as they appear in the product.
- Zoom the control the viewer must notice, but never so tight that DEMO or legal labels disappear.
- Skip anything that would leak a private account, an internal tool, or a third-party app.
- If pricing, checkout, or plan names are on hold or unapproved, do not film them.
- End on a clean end card: product name, last line, and a URL or contact the owner confirmed.

### 6. Compliance checks
- Every shot maps to a live, recordable feature from step 1.
- No invented numbers, quotes, logos, or "trusted by" lines.
- DEMO data is labelled in the data and in the frame.
- Claims stay inside wording the product or an approved brief already uses.
- Voice, music, faces, and customer work have the required sign-off.
- Legal, pricing, and private-data surfaces are reviewed or cropped out.
- Anything still planned sits in a held list, not a shot list.

### 7. Recording checklist
- The owner has approved the scripts to shoot and named the machine.
- Recorder settings: 9:16 (1080×1920), cursor polish on, auto-zoom on the click path, local MP4 export, preview watermark on until approval.
- Prefer a machine that can hide the system cursor (Linux builds of some recorders cannot).
- A dedicated demo account and a clean browser profile.
- File names include DEMO and PREVIEW until the cut is approved.
- Suggest a posting cadence that alternates audiences. The owner posts unless they clearly asked otherwise.

### 8. Output
One draft pack (Markdown is enough) with: the live vs not-yet-live inventory, the 10–15 clip sheets, a held / do-not-record list, the recording-day checklist, and open questions for the owner (voice, machine, URL, which clips go first). Keep it marked draft until recording is approved.

## When to stop
If the product is not ready to show, write the pack against what exists, flag the blockers, and wait. Never mock a flow and present it as the live product.
